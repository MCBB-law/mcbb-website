Matt Church is a partner at Manning Curtis Bradshaw & Bednar in Salt Lake City. Matt defends ERISA benefit denial claims in the United States District Court for the District of Utah, represents Utah cities, counties, and special districts against constitutional, employment, and tort claims, and litigates commercial disputes for businesses across the state.

Matt’s commercial practice covers breach of contract, business divorce and partnership disputes, non-compete and non-solicitation agreements, fraud, tortious interference, unfair competition, trade secrets, and product liability. He also maintains an active insurance coverage practice covering declaratory judgment actions, coverage opinions, bad faith claims, and policy exclusions.

Matt serves as local counsel for firms outside Utah litigating in Utah state and federal courts, and sponsors out-of-state attorneys for pro hac vice admission under the Utah Supreme Court’s rules.

Prior to joining the firm, Matt was a Shareholder with Plant, Christensen & Kanell, Legal Intern at Intermountain Healthcare, and a law clerk at Blaisdell & Church.

## ERISA Litigation

Matt defends ERISA claims in the District of Utah, with particular focus on benefit denial claims under 29 U.S.C. § 1132(a)(1)(B) involving residential treatment, wilderness therapy, and behavioral health services. Utah’s concentration of treatment facilities makes the District of Utah a frequent venue for these cases, and Matt regularly works with out-of-state plan sponsors and national benefits counsel who need Utah trial counsel here.

Representative issues include medical necessity determinations and plan-defined level of care criteria; the scope and standard of administrative review; the effect of external review determinations on subsequent plan decisions; claims under the Mental Health Parity and Addiction Equity Act; fiduciary status and delegation among plan sponsors, insurers, and third-party administrators; exhaustion of plan and state regulatory remedies; and allocation of exposure across successive plan years and successive administrators.

Matt represents plan sponsors, plan administrators, third-party administrators, and fiduciaries.

## Local Counsel for Out-of-State Firms

Matt appears as Utah counsel of record for firms outside the state, sponsors out-of-state attorneys for pro hac vice admission, and handles filing, local motion practice, hearing coverage, and depositions in Utah. The practice is not limited by subject matter. It most often arises in commercial and business disputes.

Engagements range from filing and appearance support to full participation in discovery, motion practice, and trial, depending on what lead counsel needs.

## Municipal Defense

One of Matt’s primary practice areas is civil rights claims and government defense. He has successfully defended municipalities and their employees against constitutional claims, employment disputes, personal injury accidents, land-use appeals, and premises liability claims (“slip and fall”). In addition, Matt provides training to governmental entities and their officers regarding the Utah Governmental Immunity Act, Section 1983 claims, and other liability related topics.
